Trip underway · left Sept 27, 2026 · page updated Sept 27

The plan: fly in at Triple Tree Aerodrome near Spartanburg, ride up into the mountains, stay two nights near the best roads and ride them unloaded on day loops. Each day below has the route, photos of what's out there, the story behind the roads, a sketch map, and a button that opens turn-by-turn directions.

RouteCamp

Why this route is great on a motorcycle

This corner of the Blue Ridge and the Smokies has the highest concentration of great riding roads in the East. The loop strings the best of them together and rides most of them on an unloaded bike.

  • Three of the most famous roads in America, back to backThe Tail of the Dragon, 318 curves in 11 miles. The Cherohala Skyway, 43 miles of mile-high sweepers with no towns, no gas and almost no traffic. And the highest section of the Blue Ridge Parkway, a road built for the view with no trucks, no stop signs and no billboards.
  • Curves that flowThese are mountain roads laid out along ridges and rivers, so one bend leads into the next for miles at a time. Almost none of the loop is straight, and the interstate only appears on the ride home.
  • A mile of elevationFrom under 1,000 feet in the Nantahala Gorge to over 6,000 on the Parkway. That means climbing switchbacks, ridge-top sweepers and long descents in the same afternoon, and temperatures 15 to 20 degrees cooler up top.
  • Ridden lightTwo nights in each area means the famous roads get ridden without a week of gear on the back. A G310 GS unloaded on the Dragon is a different bike from one loaded down.
  • Something to stop for every few milesWaterfalls beside the road, overlooks at every gap, the dam from The Fugitive, lunch in Tellico Plains. Three or four hours of riding fills a whole day.
  1. Day1

    Triple Tree toward Brevard

    Sun, Sept 27 · About 110 miles, 3 hours riding. No interstate.

    SC 11US 276

    Leave Triple Tree, stop at the Walmart on Woodruff Rd for trip supplies, then Cycle Gear in Greenville. Pick up SC 11, then take US 276 up the escarpment past Caesars Head. Continue through Brevard for the night.

    Walmart is at 1451 Woodruff Rd, open from 6 AM. Cycle Gear opens at 10 AM on Sundays, at 2017 Wade Hampton Blvd.

    • Walmart, Woodruff Rd for food and camp supplies.
    • Cycle Gear, Greenville for bike pads.
    • SC 11, the Cherokee Foothills Scenic Highway, along the base of the escarpment.
    • US 276 up Caesars Head: tight switchbacks and a big overlook at the top.
    • Through Brevard, then into Pisgah National Forest.

    Overnight: near Brevard.

    About these roads

    SC 11, the Cherokee Foothills Scenic Highway, runs along the base of the Blue Ridge Escarpment, the wall where the mountains drop to the Piedmont. It follows an old Cherokee trading path and stays away from the interstate the whole way, which is why it's on the route.

    US 276 climbs the escarpment to Caesars Head, about 2,000 feet in a few miles of switchbacks. Nobody agrees on the name. The rock outcrop supposedly looked like a Roman emperor, or, more likely, it was named for an early settler's dog. After a wagon road reached the top in 1848, a former state senator built a summer cottage and then a hotel up here so Lowcountry families could escape the heat. The overlook is still the stop.

    North of Brevard the road enters Pisgah National Forest, established in 1916, one of the first national forests in the East. The land was George Vanderbilt's hunting estate, and his forester Carl Schenck started the country's first forestry school here in 1898. Most of what looks like untouched forest along the Davidson River was logged bare a century ago and grew back.

  2. Day2

    Blue Ridge Parkway to Robbinsville

    Mon, Sept 28 · About 135 miles, 4 hours riding.

    Blue Ridge PkwyUS 19/74US 129

    Ride the Parkway south along its highest stretch, past Richland Balsam, all the way to its end in Cherokee. Then go through Bryson City and the Nantahala Gorge, on to Robbinsville, and set up camp at Robbinsville area.

    Check before you go: look at the NPS road status page for closures around Devil's Courthouse (MP 422). If it's closed, detour through Waynesville and Maggie Valley and rejoin the Parkway at Soco Gap.

    • Blue Ridge Parkway from US 276 south, the highest section of the whole Parkway.
    • Richland Balsam, the Parkway's highest point at 6,053 ft.
    • Parkway ends in Cherokee, then US 19/74 through the Nantahala Gorge past the rafting put-ins.
    • Fuel and supplies in Robbinsville before heading to the lake.

    Overnight: near Robbinsville, base for three nights.

    About these roads

    US 276 north of Brevard is called the Forest Heritage Scenic Byway. Looking Glass Falls sits right beside the pavement; a logging railroad ran the same line in the 1910s. A few miles on is Sliding Rock, a natural 60-foot water slide that runs 11,000 gallons a minute, and then the Cradle of Forestry at the site of the old forestry school.

    The Blue Ridge Parkway was a Depression-era public works project. Construction began in 1935 and the 469-mile road wasn't finished until 1987, when the Linn Cove Viaduct closed the last gap. It has no stop signs, no trucks and no billboards. Jerome joins it at Wagon Road Gap and rides the highest section: past Graveyard Fields, named for the moss-covered stumps a 1925 fire left behind, past Devil's Courthouse, where Cherokee legend says the slant-eyed giant Judaculla held court in a cave, and over Richland Balsam at 6,053 feet, the highest point on the entire Parkway. Then it drops nearly 4,000 feet to Cherokee.

    Cherokee is the seat of the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians, the descendants of people who hid in these mountains and avoided the Trail of Tears in 1838. From there US 19/74 runs through the Nantahala Gorge. The Cherokee name means land of the noonday sun, because the gorge is so deep sunlight only reaches the river around midday. Olympic paddlers train on this water. Lake Santeetlah was created in 1928 when Alcoa dammed the Cheoah River to power its aluminum smelters.

  3. Day3

    Cherohala out and back

    Tue, Sept 29 · About 110 miles, 3.5 hours riding. Unloaded bike.

    Cherohala SkywayRiver Rd

    Ride the Skyway west to Tellico Plains and have lunch in town. Then take River Rd to Bald River Falls, which you can see right from the road. Ride back over the Skyway to camp.

    This is the one road you ride both ways, and it's just as good going east.

    • Cherohala Skyway: 43 miles, no towns, no gas, up to 5,400 ft. Fill up in Robbinsville.
    • Lunch in Tellico Plains.
    • Bald River Falls, a 90-ft waterfall visible right from the bridge on River Rd.
    • Skyway again on the way back, with the afternoon light the other way.

    Overnight: back near Robbinsville.

    About these roads

    The Cherohala Skyway began as a joke. In 1958 a Tellico Plains businessman named Sam Williams, a fan of the TV show Wagon Train, suggested at a Kiwanis meeting that they run an actual wagon train over the mountains to Robbinsville to draw attention to how badly the two towns needed a road. They did it, on old logging roads, and kept doing it every year. It took 34 years and about $100 million before the road opened in October 1996. It's the most expensive highway North Carolina ever built.

    The name joins the two national forests it crosses: the Cherokee in Tennessee and the Nantahala in North Carolina. Forty-three miles, no towns, no gas, no houses, and most of it above 4,000 feet. The high point is 5,390 feet at Santeetlah Overlook, near the state line. Near Stratton Ridge is a memorial to nine airmen killed when a USAF plane crashed here in a storm in 1982.

    At the Tennessee end, River Road follows the Tellico River to Bald River Falls, roughly 90 feet, visible straight from the bridge. On the North Carolina end, a few miles on is Joyce Kilmer Memorial Forest, set aside in 1936 for the poet who wrote "Trees" and was killed in World War I. It's one of the last stands of virgin cove hardwood in the East, with tulip poplars over 400 years old and 20 feet around.

  4. Day4

    The Dragon and the Smokies

    Wed, Sept 30 · About 170 miles, 5.5 hours riding. This is the big day, so start early.

    US 129 DragonFoothills PkwyUS 321US 441NC 143

    Fuel up at Deals Gap, then ride the Dragon north. That's your only pass. Pick up the Foothills Parkway to Walland, then 321 to Townsend. Take Little River Rd to Sugarlands, then 441 south over Newfound Gap to Cherokee. Head back through Bryson City and NC 143 to camp.

    In the Smokies you need a parking tag if you park longer than 15 minutes.

    • Tail of the Dragon: 318 curves in 11 miles. Ridden once, northbound, on an unloaded bike.
    • Foothills Parkway, a quiet ridge-top road with views back over the Smokies.
    • Townsend, the quiet side of the Smokies, then Little River Rd along the water.
    • Newfound Gap on US 441: over 5,000 ft, crossing back into North Carolina on the state line.
    • Loop closes through Cherokee, Bryson City and NC 143.

    Overnight: back near Robbinsville, last night here.

    About these roads

    The Tail of the Dragon is the 11-mile stretch of US 129 from Deals Gap on the state line down to Calderwood Lake in Tennessee: 318 curves, no driveways, no side roads, and the Smokies on one side and Cherokee National Forest on the other. It's the most famous motorcycle road in the country. Riders come from everywhere, so Jerome rides it once, early, on an unloaded bike, and leaves the rest of the day to quieter roads. Just below the gap is Cheoah Dam, built in 1919. Harrison Ford's character jumped off it in The Fugitive.

    The Foothills Parkway is the road that took the longest to build in National Park Service history. Congress authorized it in 1944. Money came and went, a section called the Missing Link sat unfinished for decades because of a gorge that kept collapsing, and the whole thing finally opened in November 2018. It runs the crest of Chilhowee Mountain with the Smokies laid out beside it.

    Townsend calls itself the peaceful side of the Smokies. Little River Road follows the river into the park along an old logging railroad grade; the Little River Lumber Company cut most of these mountains before the park existed. US 441 climbs to Newfound Gap, 5,048 feet, on the state line. Arnold Guyot found it in 1872 while measuring the range, and it replaced the old Indian Gap road when the highway opened in 1932. The stone Rockefeller Memorial at the top thanks the family for the $5 million that finished buying the land; Franklin Roosevelt stood here to dedicate the park in September 1940. Great Smoky Mountains is the most visited national park in the country, and the blue haze it's named for is real: the trees release compounds that scatter light.

  5. Day5

    Moonshiner 28 and the Cullasaja Gorge

    Thu, Oct 1 · About 170 miles, 5.5 hours riding. Break camp.

    NC 28US 64

    Start at Deals Gap and ride 28 past Fontana and Stecoah to Franklin. Then go through the Cullasaja Gorge to Highlands, and on through Cashiers and down US 64 to Brevard and Brevard area.

    Cullasaja Falls only has a small roadside pull-off, so watch for it.

    • Moonshiner 28, the old bootlegging road along Fontana Lake.
    • Fontana Dam, the tallest dam in the eastern US, right beside the road.
    • Cullasaja Gorge on US 64: the road is cut into the cliff, with Dry Falls and Bridal Veil Falls near Highlands.
    • Highlands and Cashiers, then down the escarpment to Brevard.

    Overnight: back near Brevard. Loaded bike all day, so an easier pace.

    About these roads

    NC 28 from Deals Gap to Franklin is called Moonshiner 28. During Prohibition this was whiskey country, and the road along Fontana Lake was a run route for bootleggers heading to the towns. Today it's a mix of tight, wooded corners and long views over the water.

    Fontana Dam is beside the road. The Tennessee Valley Authority built it in 36 months during World War II, working around the clock, to power Alcoa's aluminum plants and the secret city at Oak Ridge. Five thousand workers lived in a village built for the job. At 480 feet it's the tallest dam east of the Rockies, and the Appalachian Trail walks straight across the top of it. Several towns and a road disappeared under the lake; the road was never rebuilt, which is why the far shore is empty.

    Franklin sits in the Cowee Valley, where rubies and sapphires turn up in the gravel. Above it, US 64 climbs the Cullasaja Gorge with the road blasted into the cliff, waterfalls beside and below it: Cullasaja Falls, then Dry Falls, which you can walk behind, then Bridal Veil Falls, which US 64 ran directly under until 1959. Highlands, at 4,118 feet, is one of the highest towns in the East. Two Kansas developers founded it in 1875 after drawing a line on a map from Chicago to Savannah and one from New York to New Orleans and deciding the spot where they crossed would become a great trading center. It didn't, but it became a summer resort. From Cashiers the road drops off the escarpment to Brevard.

  6. Day6

    Flex day near Brevard

    Fri, Oct 2 · Ride as much or as little as you like.

    Rest, go for or take a short spin on the Parkway.

    • Sleep in.
    • A slower morning after five days of riding.
    • Maybe a short spin up to the Parkway, or a coffee in Brevard.

    Overnight: near Brevard, last night of the trip.

    About these roads

    A slower day to rest the legs and the bike. Bent Creek, nearby, is the oldest experimental forest in the East; the Forest Service has been studying how these mountains grow back since 1925.

    Mount Pisgah, the peak with the TV tower, was named by a preacher in the 1700s after the mountain where Moses looked out at the promised land. George Vanderbilt built a hunting lodge just below the summit and rode up from Biltmore on a private trail; the Parkway now runs past the site, and the Pisgah Inn at 5,000 feet has one of the best porches on the road.

    Home to Charleston

    Sat, Oct 3 · About 4 hours home. Scenic roads, then interstate.

    US 276I-26

    Ride scenic roads down out of the mountains toward Spartanburg. Once the fun riding is done, hop on I-26 and run straight into Charleston.

    • Down out of the mountains on the scenic route toward Spartanburg.
    • I-26 from there straight into Charleston. Home by afternoon.

    Tonight: Home in Charleston.

    About these roads

    Down out of the mountains toward Spartanburg, then I-26 across the state. About 200 miles, mostly flat, ending on the Ravenel Bridge over the Cooper River, which opened in 2005 as the longest cable-stayed bridge in North America. Home in Charleston by afternoon, about 1,300 miles after leaving.
